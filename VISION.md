# Vision

> Hardware is temporary. Knowledge is permanent.

Project Aletheia aims to become a private, durable personal AI system that helps its user understand information, preserve knowledge, and carry work forward over time.

“Let nothing remain concealed” expresses a commitment to understanding, traceable reasoning, and explicit uncertainty. It does not authorize disclosure of private information.

## Knowledge that outlasts tools

The lasting asset is the user's knowledge: source material, context, decisions, and the reasons behind them. Models and interfaces should be replaceable working components.

Important records should remain understandable outside the application that created them. Portable formats such as Markdown, JSON, YAML, CSV, and standard databases are preferred where appropriate. Source provenance and migration procedures should travel with the records.

The intended knowledge workflow should remain compatible with an Obsidian-based knowledge base and user-controlled archival storage. A hosted assistant's memory is a working aid; durable records belong in the user's controlled knowledge store.

## Private intelligence

Local-first operation is the intended default. A future local or offline system should be able to use retained knowledge without requiring a particular cloud provider.

Any use of external services should have an explicit purpose, a known data boundary, and a practical exit path. Offline support remains a goal until its actual scope and limitations have been tested.

## Governed autonomy

Aletheia should assist within permissions chosen by the user. As action-taking capabilities are introduced, they should have defined limits, inspectable records, and appropriate human approval for consequential actions.

Retrieved documents and external content should be treated as evidence, not as authority to change permissions or execute instructions.

## Intended capabilities

Areas to explore include source-backed retrieval, durable decision records, continuity across sessions, and bounded assistance with workflows. These are aspirations, not a feature inventory.

Success would mean that the user can inspect an answer's sources, recover important knowledge after a component failure, and migrate to replacement tools without rebuilding the knowledge base from scratch.

Progress toward that vision should be supported by documented tests and limitations. See the [roadmap](ROADMAP.md) and [principles](PRINCIPLES.md).
