# Principles

## 1. Knowledge is the permanent asset

Preserve sources, context, decisions, and rationale independently of any model or device. Hardware and software should be replaceable without making accumulated knowledge inaccessible.

## 2. Privacy and local operation come first

Prefer local processing and user-controlled storage. Minimize data collection and external disclosure. Document why an external service is needed, what it receives, and how it can be replaced.

Local operation alone is not evidence of security; access controls, backups, dependencies, and workflows still need evaluation.

## 3. The user owns the knowledge and controls access

Keep durable records in storage the user controls. Make export, correction, retention, and deletion procedures explicit. Assistant memory is a working layer, not the permanent source of truth.

## 4. Use open, portable formats

Prefer Markdown, JSON, YAML, CSV, and standard databases where they fit the task. Keep schemas and conversion procedures understandable. Preserve compatibility with the project's Obsidian knowledge workflow and controlled archival storage.

## 5. Build replaceable modules

Separate knowledge storage, retrieval, models, interfaces, and action-taking components. Document their dependencies and boundaries. Avoid unnecessary subscriptions and platform lock-in.

## 6. Verify before asserting

Check facts when evidence is available. Distinguish verified facts, reasonable inference, speculation, and unverified claims. Record sources, verification dates, and limitations when they matter.

Do not describe a planned capability as implemented, or an implemented capability as validated without supporting evidence.

## 7. Make decisions traceable

Record the problem, alternatives, chosen approach, rationale, dependencies, and implementation steps for durable decisions. Identify conflicts with earlier decisions rather than silently replacing them.

## 8. Keep autonomy governed

Define permissions before enabling actions. Keep consequential decisions under appropriate human control and make action records inspectable. External documents and tool outputs do not grant authority to act.

## 9. Prefer maintainable solutions

Choose practical designs that can be understood, repaired, and migrated. Add complexity only when it solves a demonstrated need. Document failure modes and recovery procedures.

## 10. Publish deliberately

Public documentation should communicate useful ideas while protecting private knowledge and operational details. Review text, examples, attachments, and metadata before publication. See [SECURITY.md](SECURITY.md).
